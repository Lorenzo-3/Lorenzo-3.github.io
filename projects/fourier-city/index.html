Overview

One signal, two domains, one city

Fourier City makes signal processing visible. The live waveform shows how amplitude changes over time; the skyline shows how energy is spread across frequencies. Change the sound with controls in the scene and compare both views straight away.

Try generated sine, square, triangle, sawtooth, rich-harmonic, and noise signals, or play the bundled MP3 or one of your own. Adjust pitch, cutoff, gain, and resonance directly on the 3D desk.

Control desk

Physical controls, exact feedback

Use the buttons to choose sources and filters. The knobs turn through 270°, updating their Hz, Q, dB, or playback-rate labels as you move them.

Time domain

Live waveform

A glass display plots 2,048 post-filter samples. It starts near a rising zero crossing to keep periodic waveforms visually stable.

Frequency domain

Audio-reactive skyline

Position and hue show frequency. Building height, line brightness, and ground motion show spectral energy.

Pitch

Hierarchical robot

For periodic signals, a two-link robotic arm points to the skyline band that matches the selected pitch and follows the building’s roof.

Audio pipeline

One processed signal drives sound and graphics

MP3 buffers and procedural sources use the same controller. Live oscillators let periodic waveforms change pitch smoothly; noise and music use looping buffers. The selected biquad filter sits before analysis and positional output.

Logarithmic pitch

For periodic oscillators, the normalized knob value maps evenly across the audible range.

f(p) = 20 × (20000 / 20)p Hz, 0 ≤ p ≤ 1

For music, pitch instead maps to a playback rate between 0.5× and 1.5×. Noise has no single pitch.

Filters with visible consequences

Cutoff ranges from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, and resonance from Q = 0.1 to 20. The peaking filter adds gain from -18 to +18 dB.

The red skyline curve predicts attenuation or emphasis. The yellow marker shows the cutoff.

Spectrum city

From FFT bins to an animated skyline

A 2,048-point FFT is sampled at 30 Hz, then grouped into 70 logarithmic bands from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Each band’s mean decibels are normalized to a shared energy value that drives the buildings, wireframes, and shader-driven ground flow.

Frequency range20 Hz – 20 kHz
Skyline bands70
Analysis rate30 Hz

Motion without jitter

Separate exponential rates let buildings rise quickly with increasing energy and fall more slowly as it decays. The solid building and its colored wireframe use the same smoothed scale.

α = 1 - e-rΔt · rise r = 200 · decay r = 16

One energy value, three visual channels

Band energy sets building height and wireframe brightness. It also feeds a compact 70-texel texture read by the ground shader. Color keeps each frequency recognizable across every layer.

Hierarchical animation

A robot arm that physically points to pitch

The arm is built as a nested Three.js hierarchy. Base yaw sets the movement plane; the shoulder and elbow solve the reach; the wrist compensates to keep the stylus oriented. Each child inherits the transformations of the joints above it.

Root→Base yaw→Shoulder→Elbow→Wrist→Stylus

Periodic oscillator pitch is rounded to the nearest skyline band. MP3 playback rate and noise don’t correspond to a single stable frequency, so the robot smoothly folds back into its inactive pose.

Two-link inverse kinematics

Before solving the joint angles, the target distance is clamped to the arm’s reachable interval. This prevents invalid poses when the selected building is beyond its exact reach.

θ₂ = acos((d² - L₁² - L₂²) / (2L₁L₂)) θ₁ = atan2(y, x) - atan2(L₂sinθ₂, L₁ + L₂cosθ₂) wrist = -(θ₁ + θ₂)

Engineering

Keeping the explanation responsive

The performance optimizations are chosen with the explanation in mind: the scene needs to stay clear while updating continuously with the audio.

Geometry

Instanced skyline

The original Blender skyscraper is normalized and reused through instancing. Expanded line buffers keep every wireframe in sync with its solid building.

Memory

Reusable buffers

Typed arrays, analyser buffers, matrices, and waveform geometry are updated in place, avoiding allocations on each frame.

Rendering

Shader-driven flow

A small RGBA texture sends band energy to a polar ground shader, which adds traveling waves, Voronoi edges, Fresnel response, and specular highlights.

Interaction

Resilient state changes

Versioned audio requests discard stale MP3 callbacks. Analytic collisions, pointer-lock recovery, and audio setup on demand help keep the experience predictable.

Next steps: add band-stop filters, microphone and streamed sources, selectable FFT sizes, keyboard-accessible controls, saved presets, and modes with less detail for weaker devices.
